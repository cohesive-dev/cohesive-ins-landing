## Content boundaries and official sources

- Pool maintenance, equipment repairs, resurfacing and construction are distinct operations. New pages request the actual work rather than treating a page category as insurer eligibility. Construction pages address gunite, excavation, work in progress and subcontractors; tree pages address removal, heights, utility lines, rigging and equipment.
- Cleaning pages distinguish GL limits from coverage for the customer's surface or item being cleaned. Damage-to-work, care/custody/control and workmanship restrictions require review of the actual policy. No universal cleaning-property coverage promise or invented premium is published.
- Texas: [Comptroller cleaning-service guidance](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/publications/94-111.php) informs invoicing questions, with exemptions kept separate from coverage. [TDI employer guidance](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/pubs/consumer/cb030.html) informs staffing review. [Austin residential tree resources](https://www.austintexas.gov/development-services/trees-residential-property) are labeled local, not statewide.
- Florida: [DBPR construction FAQs](https://www2.myfloridalicense.com/construction-industry/faqs/) support checking the actual pool-service/construction credential; no blanket maintenance exemption is inferred. [Revenue cleaning guidance](https://floridarevenue.com/Forms_library/current/brochure/gt800015.pdf) is scoped to nonresidential cleaning and its exceptions. [Workers' compensation requirements](https://myfloridacfo.com/division/wc/employer/coverage-requirements) distinguish construction from nonconstruction without classifying every cleaner. [Tampa tree resources](https://www.tampa.gov/construction-services/tree-permitting/tree-resources) do not imply every Florida removal needs a permit.
- Arizona: [ROC classifications](https://roc.az.gov/license-classifications) distinguish pool construction and minor service scope; [Industrial Commission employer guidance](https://www.azica.gov/employers-0) supports the staffing checklist. [Agriculture FAQs](https://agriculture.az.gov/frequently-asked-questions) and its native-plant program establish that protected plants can require a separate review, not that every tree is protected. Some AZ pages reject direct automated retrieval; official indexed text was checked, and no numeric licensing or permit threshold was inferred from access failures.
- North Carolina: [Industrial Commission employer guidance](https://www.ic.nc.gov/workers-compensation-claims/employers), [public-pool rules](https://www.dph.ncdhhs.gov/media/1809/open) and [GC classifications](https://www.nclbgc.org/classifications-and-limitations/) are scoped to the relevant staffing, facility and work. Public-pool requirements are not presented as rules for every backyard pool.
- Georgia: [SBWC employer FAQ](https://sbwc.georgia.gov/frequently-asked-questions/workers-compensation-insurance-faqs), [DPH public pools](https://dph.georgia.gov/environmental-health/pools) and [specialty-contractor policies](https://sos.ga.gov/page/traditional-specialty-contractors-policy-statements) support distinct checks. The last source is not claimed to establish a universal pool-contractor exemption. [Atlanta planning arborist resources](https://www.atlantaga.gov/government/departments/city-planning/nature-urban-ecology/arborist) are explicitly about local private-property review.

Official resources were checked September 14, 2026. Practical examples and quote-preparation questions are editorial guidance, not measured customer cases or carrier commitments. No starting premium, guaranteed acceptance, ranking promise or new licensing footprint is asserted.
